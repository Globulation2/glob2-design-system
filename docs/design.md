# Design principles

Make Glob2 feel alive, friendly and capable. Let real globs and the game's world
provide character. Use soft corners, warm gold actions and clear, readable surfaces.
Meadow pairs forest-green ink with pale paper. Night colony pairs cream ink with
aubergine surfaces. Both are first-class designs, not inverted copies.

Use Glob2 Sans for headings and important numbers; Nunito for reading and controls.
Keep headings clear and functional. Body copy should explain what the reader can do,
using concrete verbs and the game's established terms. Write errors with a useful next
step. Explain empty states honestly; do not invent activity or promise unsupported features.

A marketing page can have a spacious hero and editorial rhythm; an application can
have compact controls and dense workspaces. Share the palette, type, shapes, controls,
artwork and interaction states, rather than forcing the same page layout everywhere.
Use token-based size/spacing variations and document deliberate presentation differences.

Use gold for a page's primary action. Statuses need words and icons as well as color.
Keep artwork decorative unless its content carries meaning. Use the actual colony
recording rather than fabricated gameplay. Motion must never block reading or input.
